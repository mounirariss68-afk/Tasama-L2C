## 1. Tasama's business model

### 1.1 Ownership and origin
- Tasama (working names "UniCo"/"Project UNICO" — Arabic مشروع الدمج, "the merger project" — and "BIAC") was "established in 2024 as a merger between BIAC and PIF SSC with an objective to be a key player in business services". BIAC is "a 2017 TAQNIA subsidiary (PIF-owned)". PIF SSC is the "PIF Shared Services Centre for Portfolio Companies". A task force was formed "Based on HE the Governor direction to study and assess the feasibility of consolidating shared-services portfolio companies". [ONB slide 3; CSA slide 27]
- Rationale (pre-merger): SSC "was subject to a slower and more rigorous governance" and had "the inability to commercialize services"; it "utilized various partners to commercialize services including BIAC, Deloitte, EY, AfCo, SITE and STCS". UniCo was created so "partners [can] have a single commercial relationship with Unico" and "Portfolio companies then access the full ecosystem… through a single MSA with Unico", with "direct access to PIF investments such as ServiceNow, CoBuilder App, Oracle MT and the Company Builder Methodology". [TP24 §Background]
- Integration entities named: from BIAC — "Innovation Program Management, Shared Services, BIAC Spaces, International Partnerships"; from PIF SSC — "Company Builder Program, Company-in-a-Box Operations, Strategic Shared Services, Digital Enablement & Experience, Transformation Management, Corporate Property Management". [ONB slide 3; CSA slide 27]
- Regulatory context quoted: Saudization "75%" labour-law requirement; PIF audit requirements "monitored through the Transition Office"; consolidated financial reporting to PIF "currently using the Hyperion system"; data residency ("platforms are hosted within KSA"). [TP24 TPSP5]

### 1.2 Vision, mission, aspirations, themes
- Vision: "A thriving economy elevated by innovation and excellence in business services". Mission: "Empower businesses and government institutions by delivering reliable, agile and digitally native business services, enabling them to focus on their core and remain future ready". [STRAT25 slide 2]
- Aspirations (verbatim): "Be the benchmark for business services in KSA & market maker with sector expertise…"; "Be an ecosystem builder and orchestrator of economic growth in the KSA, promoting local content"; "Provide integrated suite of reliable and agile business services empowered by strong partnerships"; "Offer digitally native service portfolio with cloud-based, analytics and marketplaces"; "Develop and own IP across technologies, platforms and service capabilities"; "Be the trusted business partner to key public and private accounts, beyond the PIF portfolio"; "Be the preferred managed services provider for Government efficiency program and flagship events through 2034". [STRAT25 slide 2]
- Four strategic themes: "CUSTOMER VALUE DRIVEN – Unlock new frontiers while scaling the base"; "MOST ADMIRED BRAND – Activate brand and uplift market positioning"; "BEST PLACE TO WORK – Build high-performance teams and foster innovation"; "MARKET DISRUPTOR – Harness technology and partnerships for operational excellence". 23 initiatives across objectives A.1–D.4. [STRAT25 slides 3–9]
- Three waves: Wave 1 (by 2026) "Primary business services provider to PIF entities across key domains", customer mix PIF 90%, service mix BE 50% / SES 40% / EVAS 10%; Wave 2 (by 2028) "Mature, tech-enabled business services player in KSA with enhanced portfolio", PIF 70%, BE 35/SES 50/EVAS 15; Wave 3 (by 2030) "National champion for integrated, advanced tech and AI powered business services", PIF 50%, BE 20/SES 60/EVAS 20. Client segments: PIF "New Co / Scale up / Mature Co" and Non-PIF "Private / Public". [ONB slide 5; CSA slide 52]

### 1.3 Service layers and how clients are charged
The portfolio is organised in three layers with distinct commercial models [ONB slide 6, verbatim]:

| Layer | Description | Business model / charging | Clients |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Business Enablement (BES)** | "Enablement services and digital solutions for new enterprises/ Gov. entities (e.g., Workplace-as-a-service)" | "Providing workplace solutions and technology services to employees and corporates through fixed-fee contracts, long-term leases, and subscription" | Government Entities, Large Corporations / Multinationals, Startups and SMEs, PIF Companies |
| **Smart Enterprise Services (SES)** — "Primary Focus for Tasama" | "Standardized tech-enabled products/ platforms or bespoke services across business functions (e.g., HR, Finance, Procurement)" | "Running back-office operations at scale, reducing costs through efficiency. Charging for these services typically via fixed fees for design/ build/ advisory part, and volume-based for managed services part" | same |
| **Enterprise Value-Added Services (EVAS)** | "Front-office services that help customers achieve competitive advantage (e.g., innovation, data analytics, customer value mgmt.)" | "Premium design/ build and managed services… Charged through subscriptions, value/ outcome-based pricing, tech solutions, and advisory fees" | same |

Core service components by layer [STRAT25 slide 13]: EVAS — Innovation Program Mgmt., Marketing, Data Analytics and AI, Customer Experience & Value Mgmt.; SES — Finance, Accounting & Tax; Human Capital & Talent Search; Procurement & Supply Chain; MPO ("Deploy and manage on-demand workforce"; "Provides skilled personnel through outsourced staffing"); GRC & Legal; Customer Management; Transformation as a Service; BES — Workplace as a Service; Digital Enablement. The Onboarding Pack adds "Company Builder Playbook" as a 14th area and states "Tasama currently categorizes its services under 14 service areas, 9 of which includes services that are being actively delivered". [ONB slide 8]

Pricing / commercial-model evidence:
- D&T defined six commercial models for its services: "Time & Material", "Fixed Fee Model", "Subscription-Based", "Value / Outcome-based", "Consumption-Based", "Dedicated Resource / Capacity Model", plus "A hybrid model… combining two or more". Each D&T service is mapped to applicable models (e.g., Service Request Fulfilment: subscription for managed desks, consumption "for standardized and repetitive set of services (e.g., machine initialization)", T&M for ad hoc, dedicated-resource for "full time IT support"; User Onboarding "Consumption-Based – common when priced per new hire onboarding"; ERP/CRM "Fixed Fee – for the initial configuration /setup" + "Time & Material – for additional support (i.e., customizations)"). [PORT slides 42–62]
- Finance service charters reuse the same six-model palette per service (O2C charter shows the model chips; "O2C Commercial Model Rationale" slide is blank/WIP). [FIN-CHART slides 14–16]
- Observed D&T revenue shapes in the PO ledger: AMS billed monthly against a 12-unit PO (e.g., JADA "General operation and support services" 12 × SAR 52,500; "Oracle Application Support & Mgmt. Services" 12 × ~SAR 58,583); staff-augmentation rate cards ("Functional Specialist (Finance, Procurement, EPM, HCM, CX) - Onshore (Rate 4312)"); milestone fixed fees ("Implementation & Engagement Management & Governance cost to be Paid at Production sign-off" SAR 2,447,200 / "…at Go-Live" SAR 1,223,600); licence resale ("Licenses for Oracle" SAR 1,388,610); hardware line items (docks, Apple Pencil). Billing is gated by "DN Sign-off Status" / "Rq 4 CoC Sent" (Certificate of Completion), and tracked with "Collection Status (Y/N)", "Open PO", "KAM", "Delivery". [PO-SUM]
- Pain point stated by Sales: "Rigid Commercial Models — The current commercial models are rigid and inflexible, preventing from being tailored to individual client size and needs"; "One-size-fits-all Services". [CSA slide 12]
- Commercial governance process maps call for "pricing options (tiers, volumes, models)", "modular commercial models with adjustable pricing tiers and service levels", and note "Lack of standard commercial models" and "Lack of standard proposals, and capacity models". [CGOV]
- Historic SSC model: "Create a recharge model based on sharing configuration work, subscription pooling, shared master data management, and shared environment support" for Oracle MT. [TP24 Oracle MT initiative]
- Note: `CPQ Deal Summary.xlsx` is Publicis Sapient's own rate card to Tasama (daily SAR rates by grade, KSA/International/Offshore), not Tasama's client pricing. [CPQ Deal Summary]
